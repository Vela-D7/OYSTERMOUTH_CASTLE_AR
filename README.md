# OYSTERMOUTH_CASTLE_AR

Augmented Reality heritage reconstruction project developed as part of the **MSc Virtual Reality dissertation at Swansea University**.

This project explores how **Design Thinking and UI/UX principles can be applied to wearable Augmented Reality (AR)** systems to enhance interpretation of historic sites.

The prototype focuses on **Oystermouth Castle Chapel (Swansea, Wales)** and reconstructs medieval wall painting fragments located in the **confession area**, including:

- Two angel figures  
- Two heraldic shields  

The AR experience was developed using **Unity and XREAL Air 2 Ultra AR glasses with Beam Pro**.

# Project Overview

Historic heritage sites often rely on static interpretation methods such as signs, guidebooks, or audio tours. These approaches provide useful information but may not fully communicate the original appearance of historical environments.

Augmented Reality allows digital reconstructions to be placed directly within the physical site, enabling visitors to visualise lost or damaged architectural and artistic features.

This project reconstructs fragments of medieval artwork located in the **confession area of Oystermouth Castle Chapel**, helping visitors understand how the space may have originally appeared.

---

# AR Experience

The prototype AR system visualises reconstructed historical elements within the chapel environment.

### Confession Area Reconstruction

- Two medieval angel figures
- Two heraldic shields
- Digital overlays aligned with the chapel structure

Users wear **XREAL Air 2 Ultra AR glasses** connected to **Beam Pro**, allowing them to view reconstructed elements while standing inside the chapel.

---

# Development Process

During development, several technical approaches were explored.

### Methods Tested

1. Tablet-based AR
2. Vuforia Engine marker tracking
3. Simple UI appearance method

The **Simple UI appearance method** was selected for the final prototype and used during user testing.

The other approaches were explored during development but were not used in the final evaluation.
