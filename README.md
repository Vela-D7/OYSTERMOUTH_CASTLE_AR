# OYSTERMOUTH_CASTLE_AR
Augmented Reality heritage reconstruction project developed as part of the MSc Virtual Reality dissertation at Swansea University.

This project explores how Design Thinking and UI/UX principles can be applied to wearable Augmented Reality (AR) systems to enhance interpretation of historic sites.

The prototype focuses on Oystermouth Castle Chapel (Swansea, Wales) and reconstructs medieval wall painting fragments located in the confession area, including:

Two angel figures

Two heraldic shields

The AR experience was developed using Unity and XREAL Air 2 Ultra AR glasses with Beam Pro.
Project Overview

Historic sites often rely on static interpretation tools such as signs, guidebooks, or audio tours. Augmented Reality allows digital reconstructions to be visualised directly within the physical environment, helping visitors better understand lost or damaged historical features.

This project reconstructs fragments of medieval artwork within the chapel using AR overlays aligned with the physical architecture.

Oystermouth Castle itself dates back to the 12th century and later expansions in the medieval period and is a major heritage site in Swansea.

Project Goals

The objectives of this research project were:

Explore AR technologies for heritage interpretation

Apply Design Thinking methodology during development

Design a clear and accessible AR user interface

Prototype a wearable AR experience using XREAL glasses

Evaluate the system through user testing with 10 participants

AR Experience

The AR prototype visualises reconstructed historical elements inside the chapel:

Confession Area Reconstruction

Two medieval angels

Two heraldic shields

Spatially aligned visualisation within the chapel structure

Users wear XREAL Air 2 Ultra AR glasses, allowing them to view digital overlays while standing in the physical location.

Development Process

The development process explored several technical approaches.

Tested Approaches

Tablet-based AR

Vuforia Engine marker tracking

Simple UI appearance method

Only the Simple UI Appearance method was used in the final user evaluation.

The other approaches were tested during development but were not included in the final experiment.

User Evaluation

User testing was conducted with 10 participants.

Participants were asked to:

Wear the AR glasses

Observe the reconstructed elements

Complete a short usability questionnaire

Evaluation focused on:

usability

clarity of visualisation

perceived educational value
